Split The G: score your sip with your phone

Splitting the G is one of the best-loved little rituals in pub culture. You take a single sip from a fresh pint of stout and try to stop with the line between the dark beer and its creamy head running right through the middle of the G printed on the glass. Too timid and the line sits above the letter. Too greedy and it drops below. There's no second go: the first sip is the only one that counts.

The trouble is that every split is an argument. Was that dead centre, or a touch high? Split The G settles it. Point your phone camera at the glass after your sip and the app finds the rim, the base and the liquid line on its own, then gives you a score out of 100 and tells you how many millimetres you were off and in which direction. It runs in your web browser on iPhone and Android, there is nothing to install, and it works with any pint glass, branded or not.

How to use it

  1. Get your pint and let it settle. If it's stout, wait until the surge has finished and the head has formed a clean, firm line.
  2. Take one sip. Aim to bring the line down to the middle of the G. No topping up, no nudging the glass afterwards.
  3. Put the glass down and tap Score a pint. Hold your phone roughly level with the glass so the whole glass is in view.
  4. Let the app find the glass. It marks the rim and the base automatically. If it guesses wrong, drag the RIM or BASE guide into place.
  5. Capture your score. The reading updates live. When it settles, tap capture to save it to your history and share the result.

No branded glass? No problem. The G on the classic tulip pint sits about 30 percent of the way down from the rim, so the app draws a ghost G in exactly that spot on whatever you're drinking from. If you do have the real glass, switch on Official glass and the ghost disappears.

Why people use it

Mostly, it's for fun. A score turns a quick pub boast into a friendly contest, and the millimetre feedback actually helps you get better: "4 mm too low" is far more useful than a mate squinting at your glass. Your history keeps your best score, your average and your streak, so you can see whether your technique is improving. Sign in with just an email address and you can add friends with an invite link, challenge them to beat a result, and see everyone's best of the day on the daily G-off board, which resets at midnight.

It's also built to be sensible. The game is about one sip, not drinking more, and the sparkling edition lets drivers, non-drinkers and anyone under drinking age play exactly the same game with a glass of sparkling water. Camera frames are analysed on your phone and are never uploaded.

Frequently asked questions

What does splitting the G mean?

It's a pub challenge: take one sip from a fresh pint of stout and try to leave the line between the beer and its head running exactly through the middle of the G printed on the glass. Read the full story in What is Split the G?

Do I need the official glass?

No. The ghost G shows where the letter would be on the classic tulip pint, so any reasonably straight-sided glass works.

Is Split The G free?

Yes. It runs in your phone's browser, with no app store download and no account needed to score. Ads on the site help pay for hosting.

Are my photos uploaded anywhere?

No. The camera is used only to find your glass and the liquid line, and that happens on your phone. A snapshot leaves your phone only if you choose to share it. The privacy policy has the details.

Can I play without drinking alcohol?

Yes. The sparkling edition is the same game with sparkling water and an ice-blue look. Anyone can switch to it, and it's offered automatically to anyone who says they aren't of legal drinking age.

How is the score worked out?

The app measures the liquid line as a fraction of the glass height, so the size of the glass and how far away you hold the phone don't matter. A line inside the bar of the G scores a perfect 100. Every percent of the glass height you miss by costs six points. 85 and up is "So close", 60 and up is "Decent sip". There's a fuller explanation in how the app scores your sip.

Does it work on a computer?

The live camera is made for phones, but on a desktop you can use Pick photo to score a photo of a glass.
